# Meeting prep template

Headings verbatim. Drop any section with nothing in it, except "Where this left off",
which says so explicitly when there is no previous instance.

The worked example is fictional (Meridian Foundation as funder, Baraka as delivery
partner). Real prep draws on the reader's own accounts.

---

## [Meeting title], [time] [timezone]

**The line.** [One sentence: what this meeting is for, and what would make it a good use
of the time.]

*Worked example:*

> **The line.** Monthly funder check-in with Meridian. The thing that decides whether it
> goes well is whether Daniel gets a straight answer on the June variance, which he asked
> for twelve days ago and has not had.

### Who is in it

[Each person, their organisation, and the one thing that matters for this conversation.
Where the sources say nothing useful, say so.]

*Worked example:*

> - **Daniel Ochieng**, Meridian Foundation. Their programme officer for this grant. Asked
>   the variance question on 14 August and has chased it once.
> - **Grace Wanjiru**, Baraka. Joining for the delivery section. Nothing in the last month
>   of mail from her about this meeting.
> - **Sana Patel**. No history in your mail or notes, so nothing to say beyond the name.

### Where this left off

[From the previous instance: what was agreed, who owns it, and what is still open. Open
items owned by the reader come first.]

*Worked example:*

> Last session, 28 July.
>
> - **You owe Daniel** the revised results table. Sent 12 August, so this one is closed.
> - **You owe Daniel** the June variance explanation. Still open, asked again on the 14th.
> - **Grace** was to confirm the Kisumu venue by mid-August. No sign of it in mail.
> - Meridian agreed to hold the renewal decision until after this call.

Or, when there is none:

> No previous instance of this meeting in your notes, so there is no history to carry in.

### In flight with these people

[Unresolved threads from the last few weeks, with who spoke last. Two or three at most.]

*Worked example:*

> - **"Q3 disbursement question"**, Daniel, 14 August. He asked, you have not replied.
> - **"Kisumu workshop dates"**, Grace, 21 August. You replied last, waiting on her.

### Bring or decide

[Two or three concrete things, each answerable from what was gathered.]

*Worked example:*

> - Answer the variance question before he asks it. The reconciliation note is attached to
>   his 14 August thread.
> - Decide whether to raise the renewal term today or wait for the delivery review. Meridian
>   are holding, so the choice is yours to time.
> - Ask Grace for the venue confirmation in the room, since two emails have not moved it.

### Nothing found

[One line per source that was missing or empty.]

*Worked example:*

> No Drive attachments on the invite. Zoom had no summary for the July session, so the
> history above comes from the transcript.

---

**Coverage.** [N] calendar events, [N] mail threads, [N] meeting notes read.
[Anything unreachable.]

Want me to [draft the reply to Daniel before you go in / pull the full notes from July]?

---

## Notes on tone

The reader is walking to a call. Bold the names, keep the sentences short, and lead with
whatever is going to be awkward. Never characterise someone the sources have not
described. No em dashes, no en dashes. A first meeting with no history gets a short prep,
and that is the correct output rather than a failure.
